# NYSC Medical Verification System

A centralized, fraud-resistant platform for medical relocation requests.

Prepared for: NYSC National Directorate Headquarters, Abuja  
Classification: Confidential - Internal Briefing

## 1. Context

### 1.1 Problem Statement

Current medical relocation handling is vulnerable to fraud and inconsistent decisioning.

- Fake medical reports are forged or purchased.
- NYSC cannot reliably verify issuing hospitals and doctors.
- Manual workflows create inconsistent outcomes.
- Urban relocation incentives increase fraud pressure.
- There is limited auditability and weak oversight.

### 1.2 Why Current Process Fails

- No digital trail: poor lifecycle tracking and weak accountability.
- No trust chain: no strict accreditation and identity linkage.
- No behavioral data: pattern-level fraud is not detectable.
- No central oversight: state-level decisions are hard to monitor nationally.

## 2. Product Vision

Replace document trust with institutional control and behavioral intelligence.

### 2.1 Core Principles

- Controlled access: only accredited hospitals can issue reports.
- Digital-first: no paper upload workflow.
- Behavioral detection: pattern-based fraud signals.
- Patient-friendly: uncertainty routes to review, not blind rejection.
- Full auditability: every action is logged and explainable.

### 2.2 Solution Layers

1. Access Control: approved facilities and role governance.
2. Case Management: one Medical Case Token (MCT) per active corper case.
3. Identity Verification: NIN/BVN matching with escalation paths.
4. Fraud Detection: rule-based risk scoring engine.
5. Governance and Audit: dashboards, override controls, final authority routing.

## 3. Core System Design

### 3.1 Medical Case Token (MCT)

MCT is the primary case object and lifecycle anchor.

- System-generated unique ID.
- One active case per corper at any time.
- Bound to corper, hospital, doctor, and verification code.
- Activated only after call-up issuance.
- Cannot be independently created by corper, hospital, or coordinator.

### 3.2 MCT Lifecycle States

- Created
- Under Review
- Approved
- Rejected
- Closed

### 3.3 Hospital Access Model

Tier 1 (baseline lower risk):

- Federal teaching hospitals
- State general hospitals
- Military or defense hospitals
- University teaching hospitals

Tier 2 (elevated baseline risk):

- Accredited private hospitals (for example NHIS-registered or NYSC-vetted)
- Requires periodic re-accreditation
- Adds baseline risk weight

### 3.4 Referral Model

External records can inform evaluation but cannot determine approval alone.

- Re-evaluation by approved hospital is mandatory.
- Referral cases are tagged and tracked through lifecycle.
- Enhanced review applies to referral-tagged cases.

### 3.5 Verification Code Rules

- Generated by system only.
- Single-use and case-bound.
- Expires after 12 hours.
- One extension allowed with mandatory reason and logging.
- Invalidated by expiry, repeated failed entries, or case closure.

## 4. Identity, Access, and Accountability

### 4.1 Identity Verification Outcomes

- Exact match: proceed, low identity risk.
- Partial match: proceed with identity risk increment.
- No match: escalate to manual review, not immediate hard rejection.

### 4.2 Doctor Accountability Controls

- Doctor identity linked to professional registration (for example MDCN).
- Throughput monitoring for unusual spikes.
- Pattern monitoring for diagnosis clustering and repeated anomalies.
- Geographic consistency checks for issuing location.

### 4.3 Role-Based Access Control

Corper:

- Can view own case status and outcome reason.
- Cannot create reports or view other corpers.

Hospital doctor:

- Can create report only within active MCT.
- Cannot approve relocations.
- Can access only own caseload.

State coordinator:

- Can review flagged cases and submit controlled overrides.
- Override requires reason and is audited.

Abuja admin:

- National case visibility.
- Flag entities and trigger escalations.
- Oversight and reporting authority.

## 5. Fraud Engine (MVP)

Rule-based risk scoring combines multiple signals.

### 5.1 Example Risk Signals

- Geo mismatch (posting, activity, and hospital location inconsistency)
- Hospital time burst volume
- Doctor throughput spike
- Diagnosis clustering
- Phone reuse across unrelated identities
- Referral tag
- Tier 2 issuance baseline uplift
- Identity partial match
- Identity no match
- Repeated verification code failures

### 5.2 Time Burst Detection Example

Hospital-level hourly volume bands:

- Normal: 0 to 20 cases per hour
- Watch: 21 to 50
- Alert: 51 to 100
- Lockdown: above 100

### 5.3 Decision Thresholds

- Score 0-4: Auto-approve
- Score 5-9: Review required
- Score 10-14: Escalate
- Score 15+: Auto-reject (with recorded explanation)

Policy note: borderline or uncertain identity and evidence should route to review when fraud confidence is not strong.

## 6. Governance and Oversight

### 6.1 State Coordinator Override Controls

Allowed only in constrained scenarios such as:

- Exceptional camp medical situations
- Verified system outage effects
- Confirmed physical identity with documented exception basis
- Documented humanitarian grounds per approved policy

Required controls:

- Mandatory reason text
- Permanent logging (actor and timestamp)
- State-level override rate monitoring
- Automatic audit flag if override rate exceeds policy threshold

### 6.2 Abuja Admin Dashboard

Capabilities:

- Live national case monitoring
- Hospital and doctor behavior trend analysis
- Case-level explanation view
- Regulator escalation support
- Full audit export by period or case

## 7. End-to-End Flow

1. Call-up issued
2. MCT created
3. Corper visits approved hospital
4. Doctor submits report in system
5. Verification code sent and validated
6. Identity check runs
7. Fraud engine computes risk score
8. Decision engine routes outcome
9. Review, escalation, or closure with full logging

## 8. Corper Journey

1. Receive call-up and automatic case activation.
2. Visit approved hospital list.
3. Doctor records diagnosis digitally.
4. Enter one-time verification code.
5. Track case status and reasoned outcome notifications.

## 9. Expected Impact

- Major reduction in forged-report attack surface.
- End-to-end traceability for every case lifecycle.
- Faster anomaly detection for organized fraud patterns.
- Higher automated resolution for low-risk cases.
- Stronger national oversight and policy consistency.

## 10. Operational Comparison

Before:

- Paper-driven trust
- Weak identity linkage
- Manual detection and delayed decisions
- Low audit reconstruction ability

After:

- Tokenized digital case lifecycle
- Accredited institution trust chain
- Real-time risk signaling and explainable decisions
- National dashboard and durable audit trail

## 11. Roadmap

Machine learning is out of MVP scope and should follow sustained operational data collection.

Post-MVP phases can include:

- Unsupervised anomaly detection
- Dynamic risk-weight calibration
- Network-level fraud ring analysis
- NLP checks for templated clinical notes
- Automated regulator evidence packaging

## 12. Immediate Next Actions

1. Approve MVP scope (MCT, access control, fraud rules, dashboard).
2. Select pilot states (for example FCT, Lagos, Kano).
3. Finalize Tier 1 and Tier 2 hospital accreditation lists.
4. Define legal and policy basis for digital report primacy.
